# What the 3D client does with a world past its limits (T3D-091, Linux 4.20r6)

Date: 2026-11-25. Fuzz runs of the Linux 4.20r6 client against our own 3D lab server, loopback only, all content the lab's own cubes (runs `fuzz420-*`; no movement input). Linux 4.20r6 is 3D-equal to Windows 4.20r8 ([T3D-004](3d-engines-5007-512-and-403-411-2026-11-25.md#t3d-004-linux-and-mac-against-their-windows-twins)). Board row: [T3D-091](3d-capability-board.tsv).

A GameObject line carries a position (3 floats) and an optional rotation, but **no scale field**, so scale was pushed through the shape's own vertex extents and count through the number of `object` lines.

## Results **[lab]**

| Run | Pushed | Drew | Server | Client |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fuzz420-a | x = 1e3, 1e6, 1e9, −1e9, z = 1e9 | off-screen; reference view unchanged | all five ghosted | no error, no disconnect |
| fuzz420-inf | x = `inf`, x = `nan`, one finite | only the finite one | **the server dropped the inf/nan objects**; they were not ghosted | not sent to the client |
| fuzz420-big | a cube ~5000 units across | renders; the camera ends up inside it, filling the view | ghosted | fine |
| fuzz420-neg | a negative-size cube plus a normal one | both draw (the negative-size one is visible) | ghosted | fine |
| fuzz420-nan | a shape with a NaN vertex | drew as a normal cube, no corruption | ghosted | fine |
| fuzz420-100 | 100 GameObjects | all drew | 104 ghosts | fine |
| fuzz420-1000 | 1000 GameObjects | all drew | 1004 ghosts | no disconnect over 55 s |

Every run reached "Graal3D has been activated!", stayed connected and logged no error, clamp or "too many".

## Findings

- **Far coordinates:** finite extremes up to 1e9 in any axis are accepted, ghosted and drawn (off-screen at that distance). No clamp seen.
- **inf / nan positions never reach the client on this route:** our gserver drops a GameObject whose position is non-finite, so it is never ghosted. The client's own handling of a non-finite ghost was not exercised (it would need a raw ghost injected on the encrypted 3D UDP channel).
- **Shape scale:** a ~5000-unit shape renders (the view ends up inside it); a negative-size shape also renders. Neither clamps or crashes.
- **Many GameObjects:** 100 and 1000 were all created and drawn, with no ghost-count limit hit at 1000 and no disconnect. Frame rate was not measured (software GL, headless).
- **Out-of-range shape value:** a `.3ds` with a NaN vertex drew as a normal cube with no corruption.

## Verdict

On this route the 4.20r6 client is robust to the world limits that could be pushed from our own server: far finite coordinates, huge and negative shape scale, a NaN vertex and up to 1000 GameObjects were all accepted and drawn with no clamp, error or crash. The one hard guard is server-side: our gserver refuses to ghost an object with a non-finite position, so a malformed infinite/NaN coordinate from our own server never reaches the client.
